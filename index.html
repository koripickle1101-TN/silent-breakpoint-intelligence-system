Integrity Boundary

This project does not represent formal healthcare employment, production deployment, or a validated clinical, financial, compliance, or operational risk model.

Patient-to-Professional Perspective

A workflow problem can stay hidden internally while the patient experiences the consequence.

A missed handoff, unclear owner, incomplete document, or unresolved authorization may eventually become another phone call, a delayed service, a reschedule, billing confusion, staff rework, or a denial. SBI helps me practice asking what happened before that visible consequence.

What I Am Studying

Places where a workflow can become less reliable before the full impact is visible

01

Patient Intake

I look at whether demographic, insurance, eligibility, or front-end information entered the workflow accurately enough for the next step.

02

Documentation Readiness

I review whether the next person in the workflow has the information needed to move forward without stopping to investigate or recreate work.

03

Authorization Workflow

I look for aging work, unclear ownership, unresolved requirements, and missing escalation before the patient reaches the service date.

04

Workflow Handoffs

I study what can happen when responsibility changes hands without a clear owner, closed loop, or shared source of truth.

05

Manual Workarounds

I treat repeated manual fixes as a possible signal that people are compensating for a process that is not working reliably.

06

Operational Visibility

I ask whether the issue becomes visible only after it has already reached patients, staff workload, billing, or leadership reporting.

Simulated Case Study

Prior Authorization Delay

What Becomes Visible

A simulated authorization remains unresolved close to the service date, creating delay risk and repeated follow-up.

What I Trace Upstream

The modeled problem begins earlier because the aging work does not have a clearly assigned escalation owner.

Patient-Side Effect

The patient may experience uncertainty, another call, or a rescheduled service even though the original issue was internal workflow ownership.

Breakpoint Theme

Unclear ownership, aging work, manual escalation, and weak visibility.

Staff-Side Effect

Staff may repeatedly check the same case, recreate context, or manually compensate for the missing control.

Practice Response

I would model an aging tracker with a visible owner, service-date priority, and defined escalation checkpoints.

Metric I Would Watch

Authorization aging and the percentage of modeled cases escalated before the service date.

What I Learned

An authorization delay may involve a payer response, but it can also expose an internal workflow-visibility or ownership problem.

What This Project Shows

How I am learning to think through workflow problems

I am still building professional experience. SBI is evidence of how I approach a simulated healthcare operations problem: start with the downstream signal, trace the workflow backward, identify where control may have been lost, consider patient and staff impact, and stay clear about what the evidence can and cannot prove.

Portfolio Path

SBI sits after EVIS, PARCS, and DPIS in my healthcare operations portfolio.

EVIS looks at intake and eligibility. PARCS looks at prior authorization. DPIS looks at upstream denial risk. SBI looks across those workflows and asks where the first control loss occurred. Habit Audit then looks at recurring operational habits that may make those risks more likely.
