Evidence Boundary

Student-developed. Simulated. No PHI.

All cases, scores, dashboard values, examples, and downloadable materials in SBI are synthetic and created for educational portfolio use. No real patient information, PHI, employer data, payer case data, claims data, EHR data, staffing data, or financial results are used.

SBI does not represent formal healthcare employment, production deployment, or a validated clinical, coding, billing, compliance, payer, financial, medical-necessity, or operational risk model. Root-cause statements are hypotheses for practice, not proven causation.

Why This Matters to Me

The patient usually experiences the result, not the internal workflow label.

A person may experience another call, a delayed next step, a rescheduled service, a request to resend information, billing confusion, or uncertainty about what happens next. SBI helps me practice asking what earlier intake, documentation, eligibility, authorization, ownership, or handoff condition should be investigated rather than assumed.

What I Am Studying

Places where an administrative workflow may become less reliable before the full downstream effect is visible

01

Patient Access / Intake

I practice reviewing whether demographic, insurance, eligibility, or front-end information is internally consistent enough for the next simulated step.

02

Documentation Readiness

I practice asking whether the information needed for the next administrative workflow step appears present, clear, and routed. This is not a coding or clinical documentation determination.

03

Eligibility & Authorization

I practice looking for unresolved exceptions, aging work, unclear ownership, service alignment, and missing follow-up or escalation.

04

Workflow Handoffs

I study whether responsibility, next action, open issues, and receipt are clear when work moves between people, queues, or workflow stages.

05

Manual Workarounds

I treat repeated manual fixes as a possible signal for further review rather than proof that a particular system or process is defective.

06

Operational Visibility

I ask whether earlier exceptions, aging, ownership, or rework could be made visible before a later patient-facing or revenue-cycle signal appears.

Current 2026 Context

Prior authorization is changing, so SBI stays a workflow-learning project—not a payer-rule engine.

CMS-0057-F includes prior-authorization operational provisions for impacted payers beginning in 2026, while many API requirements generally begin in 2027. SBI does not reproduce payer-specific rules or determine coverage. I use prior authorization only as a simulated domain for practicing ownership, aging, documentation readiness, exception handling, and escalation.

Fictional Case Study

Prior Authorization Follow-Up

Visible Signal

A simulated authorization remains unresolved close to the planned service date, creating a need for additional follow-up.

Earlier Condition to Investigate

The fictional case does not show a clearly assigned escalation owner or visible aging trigger.

Potential Patient-Facing Effect

The patient could experience uncertainty, another call, or possible scheduling disruption if the issue remains unresolved.

Modeled Review Theme

Ownership, aging visibility, follow-up, and escalation.

Potential Staff Effect

Staff may need repeated status checks, context reconstruction, or manual follow-up.

Student Practice Control

I would model an aging view with a visible owner, planned-service-date priority, open-exception status, and escalation checkpoints.

Example Measure

Authorization aging days and the percentage of simulated exceptions with documented ownership and closure.

What I Would Not Claim

The simulation does not prove payer fault, internal fault, patient harm, financial loss, medical necessity, compliance failure, or the actual cause of a delay.

Financial Workflow Breakpoint

Payment Recovery / Reconciliation

Visible Signal

A later payer payment is reduced, reversed, or adjusted, or recovery activity appears on a simulated ERA/EOB.

Earlier Condition to Investigate

The original account, payment, recovery reason, remittance detail, or ownership trail may not yet be fully linked or reconciled.

Evidence to Review

Original synthetic account reference, prior payment, recovery amount, ERA/EOB reference, payer correspondence, posting detail, and remaining balance.

Operational Distinction

Paid ≠ correctly reconciled. A reduced payment is a financial signal; it does not explain the full underlying recovery by itself.

Potential Patient-Facing Effect

The patient could see a confusing balance, another statement, an unexplained billing change, or uncertainty about what they owe.

Student Practice Control

Trace the signal to the original transaction, review available evidence, separate facts from unanswered questions, assign ownership, document the next action, reconcile the balance, and verify closure.

Control Question

If a later payment is reduced because of an earlier account, what evidence should connect the recovery to the original transaction before the adjustment is treated as fully reconciled?

What I Would Not Claim

The simulation does not determine payer liability, contract correctness, coding accuracy, refund obligations, legal requirements, reimbursement correctness, or financial loss.

Patient-to-professional insight The patient sees the balance. The operations team has to understand the chain of transactions that produced it.

Interactive Continuous Improvement

Continuous Improvement & Process Gap Review™

Resolve the individual synthetic case, trace the workflow backward, review recurrence, propose one small modeled control change, document implementation evidence, compare pre-change and post-change patterns, and decide whether improvement is actually supported within the simulation.

Immediate resolution

Separate fixing the current case from changing the process that produced it.

Recurring-pattern review

Use recurrence as a signal for deeper investigation without treating it as proof of a systemic cause.

Small control change

Practice a sustainable, targeted improvement rather than assuming a complete redesign is required.

Post-change verification

Compare modeled pre/post evidence before concluding that the process improved.

Patient-to-professional insight The patient needs the individual problem resolved. Healthcare operations also has to determine whether the workflow that created the problem was strengthened.
